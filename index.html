About Still Present! Curated by Kader Attia

Every two years, the Berlin Biennale for Contemporary Art brings together international artists, theoreticians, and practitioners in exhibitions and interdisciplinary formats. The 12th edition, titled Still Present! and curated by Kader Attia, takes the historical and continuing legacies of colonialism, environmental devastation, and wounded collective memories as a point of departure to imagine modes of repair, solidarity, and planetary survival.

Featuring more than 80 international artists and collectives, the exhibition unfolds across six emblematic venues in Berlin. Rather than presenting static historical artifacts, Still Present! creates dynamic constellations between archival findings, decolonial interventions, and non-Western cosmologies that challenge dominant Eurocentric narratives.

“Repair is a continuous, perpetual movement. It is not an attempt to return to a mythic pristine past, but an active engagement with the scars of history, the extraction of the planet, and algorithmic confinement.” — Kader Attia

THEMATIC PILLAR I

Voices of the Unrepaired

Revisiting contested historical archives, restitution processes, and the unacknowledged psychological scars imprinted across generations by colonial violence.

THEMATIC PILLAR II

Planetary Extractivism

Interrogating ecological catastrophes, toxic legacies, and corporate resource plundering through soil research, water memory, and indigenous botanical wisdom.

THEMATIC PILLAR III

The Algorithmic Panopticon

Exposing how facial recognition, biometric surveillance, and automated state control extend imperial policing tactics into the digital present.

THEMATIC PILLAR IV

Feminisms of Resistance

Honoring matriarchal lineages, textile traditions, oral genealogies, and bodily defiance against authoritarian repression and racialized labor extraction.

Venues & Constructivist Berlin Map

6 Public Exhibition Sites

The 12th Berlin Biennale expands across six historic locations in Berlin. Click any diamond pin on the violet map or explore the venue cards below for opening hours, directions, and barrier-free access.

Click diamond markers A–F to highlight venue details
A Berlin-Mitte

KW Institute for Contemporary Art

Auguststraße 69, 10117 Berlin

Historic founding institution of the Berlin Biennale. Features four floors of installations, courtyards, and video works by Basel Abbas & Ruanne Abou-Rahme, Omer Fast, and Tuan Andrew Nguyen.

Hours: Wed–Mon 11:00–19:00, Thu 11:00–21:00 (Tue closed)
Transit: U8 Rosenthaler Platz, S1/S2/S25 Oranienburger Straße

B Moabit / Mitte

Hamburger Bahnhof – Museum für Gegenwart

Invalidenstraße 50–51, 10557 Berlin

Former neoclassical railway station housing large-scale sculptures and monumental interventions by Binta Diaw, DAAR, Forensic Architecture, Prabhakar Pachpute, and Sammy Baloji.

Hours: Tue, Wed, Fri 10:00–18:00, Thu 10:00–20:00, Sat–Sun 11:00–18:00
Transit: S+U Berlin Hauptbahnhof (5 min walk)

C Tiergarten

Akademie der Künste, Hanseatenweg

Hanseatenweg 10, 10557 Berlin

Werner Düttmann modernist complex hosting the central symposium hall, botanical archives by Uriel Orlow, and works by Tammy Nguyen, Praneet Soi, and Jihan El-Tahri.

Hours: Tue–Sun 11:00–19:00, Thu until 21:00 (Mon closed)
Transit: U9 Hansaplatz, S-Bahn Bellevue

D Pariser Platz / Brandenburg Gate

Akademie der Künste, Pariser Platz

Pariser Platz 4, 10117 Berlin

Glass-fronted pavilion overlooking Brandenburg Gate. Features Nil Yalter’s pioneer feminist video installations on guest workers and Lamia Joreige’s Beirut border topographies.

Hours: Tue–Sun 11:00–19:00 (Mon closed)
Transit: S+U Brandenburger Tor

E Lichtenberg

Stasi Headquarters. Campus for Democracy

Ruschestraße 103, Haus 7 & Haus 22, 10365 Berlin

The former GDR Ministry for State Security headquarters. Houses works investigating surveillance, toxic extraction, and state panopticons by Zach Blas, Susan Schuppli, and Mathieu Pernot.

Hours: Mon–Sun 10:00–18:00
Transit: U5 Magdalenenstraße

F Berlin-Mitte

Dekoloniale Memory Culture in the City

Wilhelmstraße 92, 10117 Berlin

Dedicated urban project space adjacent to the historical site of the 1884/85 Berlin Africa Conference, presenting counter-cartographies by Moses März and Deneth Piumakshi.

Hours: Thu–Sat 14:00–19:00
Transit: U6 Kochstraße / Checkpoint Charlie

Barrier-Free Access & Inclusion

All six exhibition venues are wheelchair accessible with elevators, step-free entrances, and accessible restrooms. Free loan wheelchairs and walking stools are available upon arrival at KW, Hamburger Bahnhof, and Akademie der Künste.

Registered assistance dogs are warmly welcome across all exhibition spaces and courtyards.

Language, Tactile & Sign Tours

We provide tactile models and audio-descriptive transcripts for blind and visually impaired visitors. Regular guided walkthroughs in German Sign Language (DGS) take place bi-weekly.

Guidebooks and exhibition wall texts are published in both Standard German, English, and Easy Language (Leichte Sprache).
